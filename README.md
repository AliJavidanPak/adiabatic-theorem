# The Adiabatic Theorem

**General Statement and Proof for Two-State Quantum Systems Using Methods from Complex Analysis**

## Overview

This bachelor's thesis studies the adiabatic theorem in quantum mechanics, with particular emphasis on its application to two-state quantum systems.

The general adiabatic approximation is developed, followed by a detailed derivation for a two-state system using methods from complex analysis. The analysis involves analytic continuation into the complex time plane, singularities and branch points, contour integration, Laurent expansions, and asymptotic methods.

The work examines how nonadiabatic transition amplitudes behave in the adiabatic limit and how their convergence is related to the structure of the Hamiltonian in the complex plane.

## Main Topics

- Adiabatic theorem in quantum mechanics
- Adiabatic approximation and nonadiabatic transitions
- Two-state quantum systems
- Analytic continuation into complex time
- Singularities and branch points
- Laurent series and contour integration
- Asymptotic analysis
- Semiclassical and adiabatic limits

## Contents

The thesis first introduces the adiabatic theorem and develops its general formulation. It then considers a two-state quantum system and derives the relevant transition amplitudes.

The main analysis uses complex-time methods to study the asymptotic behavior of the nonadiabatic transition amplitude. In particular, the contribution of relevant complex singularities is examined to obtain the convergence behavior in the adiabatic limit.
